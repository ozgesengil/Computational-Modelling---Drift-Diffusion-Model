# Computational Modelling of Perceptual Decision-Making

MATLAB implementations of drift-diffusion models (DDMs) and alternative dynamical evidence-accumulation models for investigating perceptual decision-making in single- and double-pulse random-dot motion discrimination tasks.

Developed as part of an ongoing research collaboration with Dr Sajjad Zabbah (UCL/Goldsmiths).

### Research Objective

To investigate how sensory evidence is accumulated over time and how interruptions between successive motion stimuli influence decision accuracy and response times.

### Implementations

- **Drift-Diffusion Models (DDM):** Implementations of sequential evidence accumulation towards decision boundaries.
- **Alternative dynamical models:** Modified evidence-accumulation models incorporating mechanisms such as leaky integration, preparatory processes, and temporal-gap-dependent dynamics.
- **Parameter estimation and model comparison:** Fitting candidate models to behavioural choice and reaction-time data using likelihood-based estimation and comparing model performance using information criteria (AIC/BIC).
- **Simulation and generalisation:** Simulating behavioural predictions from fitted parameters across motion coherence levels and inter-pulse intervals, including conditions not used in initial model fitting.
- **Model evaluation and visualisation:** Comparing observed and predicted response accuracy and reaction-time distributions to assess competing accounts of perceptual decision-making.

### Research Focus

The project investigates whether conventional evidence accumulation adequately explains behaviour when sensory information is temporally interrupted, or whether additional dynamical mechanisms are needed to account for how information is retained and decisions evolve across stimulus gaps.
